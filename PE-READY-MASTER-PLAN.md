# PE Ready Plus: Master Build Plan

**Created:** March 8, 2026
**Based on:** PE Deal Advisory Report evaluation
**Principle:** PE Ready EDUCATES sellers. It does not replace advisors, CPAs, or attorneys. Every module explains what to expect so the seller walks in informed — not blindsided.

---

## How This Plan Works

5 phases. Each phase is independent — you can do them in order or jump around. But Phase 1 is the cheapest win and should go first.

Each item has:
- **What it is** (plain English)
- **Why it matters** (what the seller gains)
- **Effort** (Small = half session, Medium = full session, Large = 2+ sessions)

## Phase 2: Critical New Modules

**Effort:** Large (this is the meat — 6 new modules)
**Impact:** Highest — these are the gaps that would make an M&A advisor raise an eyebrow

These are the 6 concepts the report flagged as missing. Each one is something a seller WILL encounter in a real deal. PE Ready explains what they are, how they work, and what to watch out for — so the seller isn't hearing it for the first time from the buyer's lawyer.

### Module A: Surviving the Quality of Earnings (QoE)

**What it teaches:** What a QoE report is, what the accountants will look at, what adjustments to expect, and how to prepare so there are no surprises.

**Key sections:**
- What is a QoE and why does every PE firm require one?
- The QoE process: who does it, how long it takes, what they request
- Adjusted EBITDA: how the QoE recalculates your "real" earnings
- Common adjustments (owner comp, one-time items, related-party transactions)
- Revenue quality: recurring vs. non-recurring, customer concentration flags
- Cash vs. accrual differences that catch sellers off guard
- How to prepare: get your books clean 12+ months before selling

**What PE Ready does NOT do:** We don't run a QoE. That's a CPA firm's job ($50K-$150K engagement). We make sure the seller knows what's coming so they can prepare and aren't blindsided when adjusted EBITDA comes in lower than expected.

**Interactive element:** A simple "QoE Red Flag Checker" — user answers 10-15 questions about their financials and gets a report showing likely QoE adjustments and preparation steps.

**Where it goes:** Week 2 (Deal Readiness), before the Data Room module

---

### Module B: Working Capital — The Surprise at Closing

**What it teaches:** How the working capital peg works, why it exists, and how to avoid losing money at the closing table.

**Key sections:**
- What is working capital and why does PE care about it?
- The "peg" — how it's calculated (trailing 12-month average)
- What's included (AR, AP, inventory, prepaids, accrued expenses) vs. excluded (cash and debt)
- The collar concept (a range where no adjustment happens)
- Seasonal businesses: why the measurement date matters enormously
- How PE firms negotiate the peg in their favor (shorter averages, excluding favorable items)
- The true-up process: 30-90 days post-close, accountants reconcile and money changes hands
- Real-world example: how a $500K working capital shortfall happens

**Interactive element:** A working capital estimator — user enters basic AR/AP/inventory numbers, sees what a typical peg would look like, and understands the dollar impact of deviations.

**Where it goes:** Week 2, after QoE module (they're related — QoE often sets the working capital peg)

---

### Module C: Tax Structuring — Asset Deal vs. Stock Deal

**What it teaches:** The fundamental tax question in every deal and why the difference can be 15-25% of after-tax proceeds.

**Key sections:**
- Asset sale vs. stock/equity sale — plain English explanation
- Why PE prefers asset deals (depreciation step-up)
- Why sellers prefer stock deals (capital gains vs. ordinary income)
- The Section 338(h)(10) compromise — treated as asset for tax, structured as stock for legal
- C-corp double taxation trap
- Installment sales: spreading tax over time
- Rollover equity tax deferral (when structured properly)
- State tax considerations (brief — "talk to your CPA")

**Interactive element:** A side-by-side comparison calculator — user enters deal value, sees after-tax proceeds for asset vs. stock sale. Not tax advice, just education.

**Where it goes:** Week 2, near the financial modules

---

### Module D: Reps, Warranties & Your Legal Exposure

**What it teaches:** What you're legally promising when you sell, what happens if something turns out to be wrong, and how to protect yourself.

**Key sections:**
- What are representations and warranties? (Plain English: your legally binding promises about the business)
- What reps cover: financial accuracy, legal compliance, contracts, employees, IP, environmental
- Fundamental vs. general reps (different survival periods — some last 1-2 years, some last forever)
- Knowledge qualifiers: "to the best of seller's knowledge" vs. flat reps (big difference)
- Indemnification mechanics: basket/deductible, cap, escrow holdback
- R&W Insurance (RWI): what it is, what it costs (2-4% of limit), how it reduces escrow
- Sandbagging: can the buyer sue for things they knew about before closing?
- The escrow holdback: 5-15% of purchase price held for 12-24 months

**Interactive element:** A "What Are You Promising?" checklist — user reviews common reps, flags areas where they might have exposure, and gets guidance on what to discuss with their M&A attorney.

**Where it goes:** Week 3 or Week 4, after LOI Review (natural progression from LOI to definitive agreement terms)

---

### Module E: Your Second Bite — Rollover Equity & Management Equity Plans

**What it teaches:** How rollover and management equity work, why the "second bite" can be worth more than the first sale, and what to negotiate.

**Key sections:**
- Rollover equity: what it is, typical amounts (20-30% of proceeds), tax treatment
- Why PE wants you to roll: alignment of interests, skin in the game
- Management Equity Plans (MEPs): the equity pool for management (10-15% of total)
- Vesting: time-based (4 years with 1-year cliff) vs. performance-based
- Good leaver vs. bad leaver: what happens to your equity if you leave early
- Waterfall distributions: how money flows at exit (debt → preferred → common)
- Drag-along and tag-along rights (when the PE firm sells, you sell too)
- Why the second exit often pays more than the first

**Interactive element:** A "Second Bite Calculator" — user inputs deal structure, rollover percentage, and growth assumptions. See what the second exit could look like.

**Where it goes:** Week 3 or Week 4, near Earnouts & Performance Multipliers (related topics)

---

### Module F: The Emotional Side of Selling Your Business

**What it teaches:** What every seller goes through emotionally during the deal process, and how to prepare so it doesn't kill the deal.

**Key sections:**
- Seller's remorse: 60-70% of sellers experience it during due diligence. It's normal.
- Identity crisis: "I'm not selling a business — I'm selling 20 years of my life"
- Family dynamics: spouse disagreements, children who expected to inherit, employees who feel betrayed
- Decision fatigue: 6 months of constant decisions, document requests, and negotiations
- The "What do I do Monday morning?" problem
- How to prepare: build a post-exit plan BEFORE the deal starts
- When to lean on your advisor team (and when to take a weekend off)
- Signals that you should pause vs. signals that you should push through

**Interactive element:** A "Readiness Self-Assessment" — not financial readiness, but emotional/personal readiness. Are you actually prepared to let go? Honest scoring with practical advice.

**Where it goes:** Week 1, early. Setting expectations upfront prevents deal-killing panic later.

## Phase 4: Thin Module Upgrades

**Effort:** Small to Medium each
**Impact:** Medium — brings base modules up to the quality level of the enhanced ones

These 5 existing modules need more content to match the quality of the enhanced modules:

### 4A. Add Backs / Quick Wins
**Current:** Sliders for add-back adjustments.
**Upgrade:** Teach which add-backs PE firms actually accept vs. contest vs. reject. Categories:
- Accepted: owner excess comp, one-time legal/accounting, personal expenses through the business
- Contested: "normalized" rent, below-market family salaries
- Rejected: above-market related-party transactions, revenue normalization
- Tie it to QoE: "which of your add-backs will survive the accountant's review?"

### 4B. Data Room
**Current:** Document checklist with categories.
**Upgrade:** (Mostly covered by Enhancement 17, but also add)
- Priority order: what buyers look at first
- Red flags: common data room problems that slow deals down
- Virtual data room best practices (folder structure, naming conventions, access controls)

### 4C. HoldCo Structure
**Upgrade:** Add content on:
- Why PE uses HoldCo/OpCo structures (liability isolation, tax efficiency, debt structuring)
- Where the seller's rollover equity sits in the structure
- Management company fee structures
- Simple visual diagram of a typical PE deal structure

### 4D. Top Performers
**Upgrade:** Connect to PE Screening Scorecard. Define "top performer" from PE's perspective:
- Consistent EBITDA growth (not just one good year)
- Low customer concentration
- Strong management team beyond the owner
- Recurring revenue base
- Defensible market position

### 4E. Strategy Doc Builder
**Upgrade:** Reframe as the "Value Creation Plan" — what PE firms actually want to see:
- 100-day plan (immediate post-close priorities)
- 3-5 year strategic plan tied to specific EBITDA improvement levers
- Organic growth opportunities (new markets, new products, geographic expansion)
- Acquisition opportunities (bolt-on targets that the platform could pursue)
- Margin improvement initiatives

## Phase 5: Credibility & Platform Features

**Effort:** Medium to Large
**Impact:** These turn PE Ready from "educational tool" into "industry-standard platform"

These are longer-term features. Not urgent, but they're what separates a good tool from a great one.

### 5A. PE Readiness Score
A single score that evolves as the user completes modules and enters data. Like a credit score for deal readiness. Updates in real-time. Shows on the dashboard. Breaks down by category (Financial, Operational, Legal, Emotional).

### 5B. Growth Story Builder
Help sellers articulate their growth narrative — the thing that turns a 5x deal into an 8x deal:
- Untapped markets
- Product/service expansion opportunities
- Geographic growth potential
- Platform acquisition targets
- Margin improvement roadmap

### 5C. Downloadable Deal Package
At the end of the program, the user walks away with a complete starter package:
- Anonymous Teaser (already exists)
- Draft CIM (already exists via CIM Generator)
- Company One-Pager (Enhancement 21)
- Financial Summary
- Management Presentation outline
- Data Room Index
Package it as a single downloadable ZIP. Premium feature.

### 5D. Industry-Specific Content
Extend the DD Checklist's industry-specific approach to other modules. A SaaS company's preparation is very different from a manufacturing company's. Start with 3-4 industries and expand.

### 5E. Real Deal Case Studies
3-5 anonymized case studies walking through real deals start to finish. Timeline, valuation evolution, negotiations, surprises, outcome. Nothing teaches like stories. These could be written content (not interactive).

## Rules

1. **BINGO before any code changes.** This plan is design talk. When it's time to build, we confirm the specific module before touching code.
2. **One module per session.** Don't stack. Build it, test it, commit it, move on.
3. **Educate, don't replace.** Every module explains what to expect. PE Ready does not do QoEs, tax advice, or legal work. It makes sure the seller walks in informed.
4. **Interactive elements in every new module.** Calculators, checklists, self-assessments. Not just text walls.
5. **Maintain the tone.** Direct, no-BS, practical. The "father-to-son" voice that makes PE Ready special.
