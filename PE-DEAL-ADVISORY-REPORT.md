# PE Ready Plus: Deal Advisory Evaluation Report

**Prepared by:** Claude (acting as PE Deal Advisor)
**Date:** March 8, 2026
**Purpose:** Evaluate PE Ready's curriculum against real-world PE deal processes and identify gaps, weaknesses, and opportunities

---

## Executive Summary

PE Ready is a genuinely impressive educational platform. After reading every module, every enhancement, and every planned feature — and comparing it against how real PE deals actually work — here's the bottom line:

**What PE Ready gets right (and gets right better than most):**
- The tone. This app talks to business owners the way a real advisor would — direct, no-BS, practical. The LOI Review module alone is worth the subscription.
- The interactive tools. Calculators, scorecards, and diagnostics that use the owner's real numbers — not textbook examples.
- The "father-to-son" feel. Post-Closing Reality, Time Kills Deals, Deal Killers Diagnostic — these have the voice of someone who's been through it. That's rare and valuable.

**What needs attention:**
- Several critical PE concepts are completely missing (see Section 2)
- Module ordering doesn't match how a real deal unfolds (see Section 3)
- Some modules are thin on content compared to the enhanced ones (see Section 4)
- The curriculum leans heavily toward the seller's emotional experience but is light on the financial mechanics that actually determine deal value (see Section 5)

**Overall verdict:** PE Ready is about 70% of the way to being a tool a real M&A advisor would recommend to clients. The remaining 30% is specific, addressable content gaps — not fundamental design problems.

## Section 2: Critical Missing Concepts

These are things a PE advisor would expect to see in any seller education program. Their absence would be noticed by anyone who's been through a deal.

### 2A. Quality of Earnings (QoE) — BIGGEST GAP

**What's missing:** The app mentions QoE in the Glossary and Professional Advisors module, but never actually teaches what a QoE report contains or how to prepare for one.

**Why it matters:** The QoE is the single most important financial document in any PE deal. It's where deals get re-traded or die. Every PE firm commissions one. The QoE determines the "real" EBITDA — and it almost always comes in lower than what the seller claims.

**What a QoE covers that PE Ready should teach:**
- Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation (reported vs. adjusted)
- Revenue quality analysis (recurring vs. non-recurring, customer concentration)
- Expense normalization (owner comp, one-time items, related-party transactions)
- Working capital analysis and the "peg" (the target working capital number that determines post-close adjustments)
- Earnings sustainability (are margins stable, improving, or deteriorating?)
- Cash vs. accrual differences
- Off-balance-sheet obligations

**Recommendation:** This should be its own standalone module, not a glossary entry. Call it "Surviving the Quality of Earnings" or "The QoE: What PE's Accountants Will Find." Make it interactive — let the user enter their financials and see what a QoE firm would adjust.

---

### 2B. Working Capital Peg — SECOND BIGGEST GAP

**What's missing:** The app mentions working capital in the Glossary and briefly in Asset Free, Debt Free, but never explains the working capital peg mechanism — which is where more money changes hands post-close than any other adjustment.

**Why it matters:** The working capital peg is the #1 source of post-closing disputes. It determines how much cash the seller keeps or gives back after the deal closes. Sellers who don't understand this lose $500K-$5M routinely.

**What should be taught:**
- How the peg is calculated (trailing 12-month average of net working capital)
- What's included vs. excluded (cash and debt are excluded; AR, AP, inventory, prepaids, accrued expenses are included)
- The "collar" concept (a range around the peg where no adjustment happens)
- Seasonal businesses and why the measurement date matters enormously
- How PE firms game the peg (using 3-month average vs. 12-month, excluding favorable items)
- The true-up process (30-90 days post-close, the accountants reconcile)

**Recommendation:** Either upgrade the existing Asset Free, Debt Free module or create a standalone "Working Capital: The $2M Surprise" module.

---

### 2C. Tax Structuring — Asset Deal vs. Stock Deal

**What's missing:** The app doesn't cover the fundamental tax question every seller faces: Is this an asset sale or a stock/equity sale?

**Why it matters:** The difference can be 15-25% of the after-tax proceeds. On a $20M deal, that's $3-5M. PE firms almost always prefer asset deals (they get a step-up in basis for depreciation). Sellers almost always prefer stock deals (lower tax rate, one layer of tax). This is a negotiation that happens in every single deal.

**What should be taught:**
- Asset sale: PE buys the assets, seller's entity keeps liabilities. Double taxation risk for C-corps. Seller pays ordinary income on some assets.
- Stock/equity sale: PE buys the ownership interests. Seller gets capital gains treatment. Simpler but PE gets no basis step-up.
- Section 338(h)(10) election: The compromise — treated as asset sale for tax purposes but structured as stock sale for legal purposes.
- Installment sales: Spreading recognition over time to manage tax brackets.
- Rollover equity: Tax-deferred if structured properly (Section 351 or Section 721).
- State tax considerations: Some states treat these very differently.

**Recommendation:** Create a "Tax Structuring 101" module in Week 2 (Deal Readiness). Interactive — let the user see the after-tax difference between asset and stock deals using their numbers.

---

### 2D. Representations & Warranties (Reps & Warranties)

**What's missing:** The LOI Review module mentions reps & warranties briefly, but never explains what they are, why they matter, or how R&W insurance works.

**Why it matters:** Reps & warranties are the seller's legally binding promises about the business. If any rep turns out to be false, the buyer can claw back money from escrow — or sue. This is where sellers get burned post-close.

**What should be taught:**
- What reps cover (financial accuracy, legal compliance, customer contracts, employee matters, IP ownership, environmental)
- Fundamental vs. general reps (different survival periods and caps)
- Knowledge qualifiers ("to the best of seller's knowledge" vs. flat reps)
- Indemnification mechanics (basket/deductible, cap, escrow holdback)
- R&W Insurance (RWI): Increasingly common, shifts risk to an insurer. Costs 2-4% of coverage limit. Can reduce or eliminate escrow.
- Sandbagging clauses (can buyer bring claims for things they knew about before closing?)

**Recommendation:** Expand the LOI Review module or create a standalone "Reps, Warranties & Indemnification" module. This is dense legal content but critical.

---

### 2E. Management Equity Plans (MEPs) and Rollover Equity

**What's missing:** The app covers earnouts well, and mentions rollover in the LOI module, but doesn't teach the mechanics of management equity plans — the primary financial incentive for sellers who stay on.

**Why it matters:** In most PE deals where the seller stays, rollover equity and the MEP together determine the "second bite of the apple" — which can be worth more than the first sale.

**What should be taught:**
- Rollover equity: How much to roll (typically 20-30%), tax treatment, valuation at closing
- MEPs: The equity pool reserved for management (typically 10-15% of total equity)
- Vesting schedules: Time-based (4-year with 1-year cliff) vs. performance-based
- Good leaver vs. bad leaver provisions (what happens to unvested equity if you leave)
- Waterfall distributions: How proceeds flow at exit (debt first, then preferred equity, then common)
- Drag-along and tag-along rights
- Why the "second bite" often exceeds the first check

**Recommendation:** Create a "Your Second Bite: Rollover Equity & MEPs" module in Week 2 or Week 4.

---

### 2F. The Emotional/Psychological Side of Selling

**What's partially covered:** Post-Closing Reality covers some emotional aspects (loss of control, identity shift). But the app doesn't address the emotional journey during the deal process itself.

**What real advisors tell sellers:**
- Seller's remorse: 60-70% of sellers experience it during due diligence. It's normal. Don't blow up the deal.
- Identity crisis: "I'm not just selling a business — I'm selling 20 years of my life." This is the #1 reason deals fall apart from the seller's side.
- Family dynamics: Spouse disagreements, children who expected to inherit the business, employees who feel betrayed.
- Decision fatigue: 6 months of constant decisions, document requests, and negotiations. It's exhausting.
- The "what do I do Monday morning?" problem: Many sellers have no plan for life after exit.

**Recommendation:** Either enhance Post-Closing Reality or add a "The Emotional Side of Selling" module early in Week 1. Setting expectations early prevents deal-killing panic later.

## Section 3: Module Ordering Issues

The current 4-week structure doesn't match how a real deal unfolds. Here are the sequencing problems:

### Problem 1: EBITDA Comes Too Late

EBITDA is the single most important number in any PE deal. It determines enterprise value, debt capacity, and the PE firm's returns. Currently:
- "EBITDA Explained" is module 7 in Week 1 (last in the week)
- "EBITDA Calculator" is module 1 in Week 3

**Fix:** EBITDA Explained should be module 2 or 3 in Week 1, right after the Glossary. Everything else builds on understanding EBITDA.

### Problem 2: Data Room Comes Before the User Understands What Goes In It

The Data Room module is the first module in Week 2. But at that point, the user hasn't learned about financial statements, QoE preparation, or what documents PE firms actually request.

**Fix:** Data Room should come after the user understands the financial concepts. Move it to late Week 2 or early Week 3.

### Problem 3: Deal Process Roadmap Should Be Module 1

The Deal Process Roadmap (Enhancement 13) gives users the GPS for the entire deal. Currently it's the last module in Week 1, after they've already learned about NDAs, IOIs, and LOIs in Deal Progression.

**Fix:** Make Deal Process Roadmap the very first module — or at least module 2 after the Glossary. Show them the full journey before diving into individual stages.

### Problem 4: Valuation Tools Are Scattered

The user encounters valuation-related content in at least 6 different modules across Weeks 2-3:
- Industry Multipliers
- EBITDA Calculator
- Returns Sensitivity
- Scenario Planning
- PE Screening Scorecard
- Competitive Analysis

**Fix:** Group these into a clear "Valuation" section within Week 3, with a logical flow: EBITDA Calculator → Industry Multipliers → Returns Sensitivity → Scenario Planning.

### Problem 5: Week 4 Deliverables Depend on Data Not Yet Entered

The CIM Generator, Anonymous Teaser, and IC Memo all pull from Company Profile and other modules. If users jump to Week 4 early (which they will), the outputs will be empty.

**Fix:** This is more of a UX issue than an ordering issue. Add progress gates or clear "complete these modules first" prompts before the Week 4 deliverable tools.

## Section 4: Thin Modules That Need Content Upgrades

The enhanced modules (12 enhancements) are significantly richer than the base modules. Here are the base modules that feel thin by comparison:

### 4A. Data Room (Week 2)
**Current state:** A document checklist with categories and checkboxes.
**What's missing:** No guidance on how to organize a virtual data room, no priority order for document preparation, no explanation of what buyers look at first, no "red flag" indicators for common data room problems.
**Recommendation:** Enhancement 17 (Financial Data Room Prep) is planned and would fix this. Prioritize it.

### 4B. HoldCo Structure (Week 2)
**Haven't read fully but based on PLAYGROUND description:** This teaches holding company structures. Important topic, but needs to cover:
- Why PE uses HoldCo/OpCo structures (liability isolation, tax efficiency, debt structuring)
- The difference between the acquisition entity and the operating entity
- Where the seller's rollover equity sits in the structure
- Management company fee structures

### 4C. Add Backs / Quick Wins (Week 2)
**Current state:** Sliders for add-back adjustments.
**What's missing:** Not all add-backs are created equal. PE firms categorize them:
- **Accepted add-backs:** Owner excess comp, one-time legal/accounting, personal expenses
- **Contested add-backs:** "Normalized" rent, "below-market" salary for family members
- **Rejected add-backs:** Above-market related-party transactions, revenue normalization
The module should teach which add-backs will survive the QoE.

### 4D. Top Performers (Week 3)
**Current state:** Appears to be a ranking/benchmark tool.
**What's missing:** Should tie into the PE Screening Scorecard more explicitly. What makes a "top performer" from PE's perspective is specific: consistent EBITDA growth, low customer concentration, strong management team, recurring revenue, and defensible market position.

### 4E. Strategy Doc Builder (Week 4)
**Current state:** Helps build a strategy document.
**What's missing:** This should be the "Value Creation Plan" — PE firms want to see the 100-day plan and the 3-5 year strategic plan tied to specific EBITDA improvement levers. Enhancement 4 (Value Creation Plan) covers some of this, but Strategy Doc Builder should complement it with a forward-looking narrative.

## Section 5: What Real PE Firms Care About (That the App Doesn't Emphasize Enough)

### 5A. Recurring Revenue Is King

The app mentions recurring revenue in Revenue Quality Score and a few other places, but doesn't emphasize enough how dramatically recurring revenue changes valuation. A business with 80%+ recurring revenue might get 10-15x EBITDA. The same business with 20% recurring might get 5-6x. The app should help owners understand how to shift their revenue mix before going to market.

### 5B. Customer Concentration Is the #1 Deal Killer

The app asks about customer concentration in several places (Deal Killers, Business Scorecard, PE Screening). But it doesn't teach sellers how to FIX high concentration before going to market. Practical strategies:
- Diversification timeline (12-24 months before selling)
- Sales team investment to broaden the base
- Contract extensions with key customers (locked-in revenue reduces risk)
- Revenue breakdowns that present concentration favorably

### 5C. Management Team Depth

PE firms don't buy companies — they buy management teams. The Management Scorecard is a good diagnostic, but the app doesn't help sellers build their team before going to market:
- Hire a CFO or controller 12+ months before selling
- Document institutional knowledge
- Create job descriptions and org charts
- Prove the business runs without the owner

### 5D. Clean Financial Statements

The app talks about financials abstractly but doesn't stress enough: PE firms expect GAAP-compliant financial statements, preferably reviewed or audited by a CPA firm. Many small businesses run on QuickBooks with tax-basis accounting. The gap between "how I run my books" and "what PE expects" is 3-6 months of work.

### 5E. Growth Story

PE firms don't buy the past — they buy the future. The app focuses heavily on current-state metrics (EBITDA, margins, concentration) but doesn't help sellers build a compelling growth story:
- What are the untapped markets?
- What products/services could be added?
- What geographic expansion is possible?
- What acquisitions could the platform make using your company as a base?

This is what separates a 5x deal from an 8x deal.

## Section 7: What Would Make PE Ready Credible to Deal Professionals

If you want actual M&A advisors, investment bankers, and PE firms to recommend this tool:

1. **Add a "Recommended Advisor" section** — Help users find M&A attorneys, investment bankers, and QoE firms. Potentially a revenue opportunity through referral partnerships.

2. **Industry-specific content** — The DD Checklist already does this. Extend it to other modules. A SaaS company's preparation is very different from a manufacturing company's.

3. **Real (anonymized) deal case studies** — Walk through 3-5 real deals from start to finish. Show the timeline, the valuation evolution, the negotiations, the surprises. Nothing teaches like stories.

4. **Benchmarking data** — Current market multiples by industry, current PE deal volume, average deal timelines. This could be a static quarterly update or an API integration.

5. **Downloadable deal package** — At the end of the program, the user should walk away with a complete starter package: Anonymous Teaser, Draft CIM, Company One-Pager, Financial Summary, Management Presentation, Data Room Index. You're 60% of the way there with the current deliverables.

6. **A "readiness score" that evolves** — Not just module-by-module completion, but an overall PE Readiness Score that changes as the user completes modules and enters data. Think of it as a credit score for deal readiness.

## Final Thought

PE Ready is not a toy. It's a legitimate educational tool that covers more of the PE deal process than most sellers learn in 6 months with their advisors. The base is strong. The enhancements are sophisticated. The tone is right.

The path from "good educational tool" to "industry-standard deal prep platform" is:
1. Fill the financial mechanics gaps (QoE, working capital, tax, reps)
2. Reorder the curriculum to match deal flow
3. Strengthen the thin modules
4. Add the planned enhancements in priority order

None of these are architectural problems. They're content problems — and content is what you're good at building.
